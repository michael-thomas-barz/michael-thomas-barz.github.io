Cross products

The cross product is a variant of the dot product. You input two 3-dimensional vectors, and it outputs another vector (notice -- the dot product outputs a number, but the cross product outputs a vector).

We write \(\vec{v} \times \vec{w}\) to denote the cross product of \(\vec{v}\) and \(\vec{w}.\)

The cross product, being a vector, has a length and a direction. The length of the cross product is the area of the parallelogram spanned by \(\vec{v}\) and \(\vec{w}\) (see the widget below). TODO

The direction of the cross product is always the normal to the parallelogram. This means that it is always perpendicular to that parallelogram; you can play with the widget below to see examples. TODO

The right-hand rule

There is one small issue with using the geometric interpretation just given: to any surface in 3-dimensional space, there are two normal vectors, pointing in exact opposite directions. TODO

So, which way does the dot product use? To determine it, you use the right-hand rule. TODO widget

Warning: the right hand rule implies \(\vec{v} \times \vec{w}\) is NOT the same as \(\vec{w} \times \vec{v},\) instead they are negatives, todo.
